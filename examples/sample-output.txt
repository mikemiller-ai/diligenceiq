Apple Regulatory Disclosure Evolution: FY2023–FY2025
====================================================================================================
Question: How have Apple's regulatory disclosures changed from 2023 through 2025, and what actions does management describe?
Scope:    AAPL · FY2023, FY2024, FY2025 · 10-K, 10-Q

Summary
  Apple's regulatory risk disclosures have grown materially in specificity and scope from FY2023
  through FY2025, moving from forward-looking warnings about the EU Digital Markets Act (DMA)
  compliance deadline to reporting concrete implemented changes across the App Store, iOS, iPadOS,
  and Safari in both the EU and the U.S. By FY2025, disclosures added new dimensions not present in
  FY2023: a U.S. court order prohibiting commissions on certain purchases, the Google antitrust
  ruling and its potential impact on Apple's search licensing revenue, and new U.S. tariffs on
  imports from key manufacturing countries. Management's described actions have correspondingly
  shifted from anticipatory compliance planning to active, ongoing remediation and engagement with
  regulators.

Key findings
  1. DMA Compliance: From Deadline Warning to Implemented Changes (reported; AAPL)
     In FY2023, Apple disclosed it was required to comply with the EU Digital Markets Act by March
     2024 and expected to make further business changes. By FY2024 and FY2025, the filings report
     that Apple has implemented changes to iOS, iPadOS, the App Store, and Safari in the EU,
     including new business terms, alternative fee structures, alternative distribution methods,
     alternative payment processing, and additional developer tools and APIs. The FY2024 and FY2025
     filings also note that Apple has continued to make changes to its compliance plan in response
     to feedback and engagement with the European Commission, and that the compliance plan has been
     challenged by the Commission and may be challenged further by private litigants.
     [AAPL-FY2023-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-016,
     AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-017]
  2. U.S. App Store: New Court Order Prohibiting Commissions Added in FY2025 (reported; AAPL)
     FY2023 and FY2024 disclosures noted U.S. changes to how developers communicate with consumers
     regarding alternative purchasing mechanisms. FY2025 adds a materially new disclosure: Apple is
     currently subject to a court order preventing it from imposing any commission or fee on certain
     purchases that consumers make, a specific legal constraint absent from prior-year filings.
     [AAPL-FY2023-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-016]
  3. Google Search Licensing Risk Escalates to Named Antitrust Ruling (reported; AAPL)
     All three years disclose that Apple earns revenue from licensing arrangements with other
     companies (including Google) to offer search services, and that these arrangements are subject
     to government investigations and legal proceedings. FY2025 adds a specific new development: on
     August 5, 2024, Google was found to have violated U.S. antitrust laws, and on September 2,
     2025, the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia ordered certain remedies. The filing
     notes that a reversal on appeal could result in DOJ-proposed remedies prohibiting Google from
     offering Apple commercial terms for search distribution, which could materially adversely
     affect Apple's ability to earn revenue from such arrangements. [AAPL-FY2023-10K-1A-018,
     AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-018]
  4. U.S. Antitrust Lawsuits Named Explicitly Starting FY2024 (reported; AAPL)
     FY2023 disclosed antitrust investigations in various jurisdictions and European App Store
     investigations in general terms. FY2024 and FY2025 filings specifically name civil antitrust
     lawsuits in the U.S. alleging monopolization or attempted monopolization in the markets for
     'performance smartphones' and 'smartphones' generally in violation of U.S. antitrust laws, a
     more specific and escalated disclosure. [AAPL-FY2023-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-017,
     AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-017]
  5. AI/ML Regulatory Risk Added as New Dimension in FY2024–FY2025 (analysis; AAPL)
     FY2023 disclosures do not mention artificial intelligence or machine learning in the regulatory
     compliance risk context. FY2024 adds that regulatory risks and costs may increase as Apple
     expands the use of machine learning and artificial intelligence features and must navigate new
     legal, regulatory and ethical considerations. FY2025 further notes that AI features can
     exacerbate risks of inadvertent personal data disclosure, and that novel IP claims may be
     exacerbated as AI is further integrated into products and services. [AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-016,
     AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-013, AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-018]
  6. FY2025 Adds Tariff Exposure as a New Regulatory/Trade Risk Category (reported; AAPL)
     FY2023 and FY2024 filings reference export/import regulatory risk in general terms. FY2025
     introduces a dedicated 'Tariffs and Other Measures' section in the MD&A, disclosing that
     beginning in the second quarter of 2025, new U.S. tariffs were announced on imports from China,
     India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam, and the EU, among others, and that several
     countries have imposed or threatened reciprocal tariffs. The filing also notes a U.S.
     Department of Commerce investigation under Section 232 into semiconductor imports and
     downstream products. [AAPL-FY2025-10K-MDA-001, AAPL-FY2023-10K-1A-016, AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-016]

Considerations
  - The progression from anticipated DMA compliance (FY2023) to implemented changes now under active
  Commission challenge (FY2024–FY2025) signals that Apple's App Store business model in the EU faces
  ongoing structural uncertainty. The DMA provides for significant fines and penalties for
  noncompliance, and the compliance plan may be challenged further by private litigants, creating a
  multi-front legal exposure that a diligence team should assess for potential revenue and margin
  impact. [AAPL-FY2023-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-017, AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-016,
  AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-017]
  - The FY2025 disclosure of a U.S. court order prohibiting Apple from imposing any commission or
  fee on certain purchases, combined with named U.S. civil antitrust lawsuits alleging smartphone
  market monopolization, represents a material escalation in domestic legal risk to the App Store
  revenue model that was not present in FY2023 disclosures. Diligence teams should evaluate the
  potential scope of commission revenue at risk. [AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-016, AAPL-FY2024-10K-1A-017,
  AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-017]
  - The Google antitrust ruling and associated court remedies, first disclosed in FY2025, introduce
  a specific and quantifiable risk to Apple's search licensing revenue. The filing notes that
  DOJ-proposed remedies could prohibit Google from offering Apple commercial terms for search
  distribution, and that a reversal on appeal could result in imposition of such remedies. This is a
  new, named revenue risk absent from FY2023 and FY2024 filings. [AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-017,
  AAPL-FY2025-10K-1A-018]
  - The FY2025 introduction of a dedicated tariff section in the MD&A, covering new U.S. tariffs on
  imports from China, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam, and the EU, plus a Section 232
  semiconductor investigation, signals that supply chain and cost structure risks have become a
  front-line regulatory concern. The filing states the ultimate impact remains uncertain and depends
  on several factors including additional tariffs, retaliatory measures, and the magnitude and
  duration of these measures. [AAPL-FY2025-10K-MDA-001]
